Greetings, Singers, For this week, I’d like you to reflect on some of the texts we are singing. Pasted below is the text of the *Magnificat*. Please focus especially on the opening line: “My soul doth magnify the Lord.” What does that mean to you? In the biblical story, Mary speaks these words after learning that she will bear a son, Jesus. But I’d also like you to think beyond the historical and religious context. Does this statement have meaning for you personally, in the modern world? What might it mean for a person today to “magnify” something larger than oneself? The other movement we have worked on (5) focuses on promises. Near the end of the *Magnificat*, Mary connects what is happening to her with promises made long before—to Abraham and his descendants. Those promises included the idea that Abraham’s descendants would become a people and that God would remain in relationship with them. So Mary is not simply celebrating something happening in her own life. She is placing herself within a larger story, one that stretches across generations. What does it mean to inherit a promise like that? How might it feel to believe that something happening in your own time is connected to hopes and commitments carried by people long before you? And are there promises—religious, personal, familial, or societal—that you feel you have inherited from earlier generations? As you think about these ideas, consider how they might affect the way we sing these passages—the color of the sound, the shape of the phrase, and the emotional energy behind the words. Next week we’ll start the third movement, which carries an entirely different message (I can’t wait!). *Chamber Ensemble*. Many thanks to singers who have joined us in the Chamber ensemble. I’m excited about this group and we made good progress during our brief rehearsal. We have 3 other pieces to learn for the concert, so this will certainly require some outside practice. This will work best if pitches and rhythms are learned before rehearsal so we can be making music right from the start. The original November 15 date below was superseded by the October 13 update.] Greetings, Choir, I hope you are enjoying the wonders of summer. This part of the world offers so much natural beauty, and it opens my heart simply to be among it—hearing the birds, seeing the trees and hillsides, and breathing the fresh air. One of my summer projects has been preparing for the Bloomsburg Singers’ fall concert. We have an exciting program planned, featuring two extended works with orchestra. Performing with instrumentalists is always both a pleasure and a challenge, and I think you will find this a rewarding experience. The two works were composed nearly three centuries apart. Francesco Durante’s beautiful and rhythmically vibrant Baroque *Magnificat*—long attributed to Pergolesi—dates from the early 18th century. By contrast, Karl Jenkins’s *Te Deum*, composed in 2008, combines strings with trumpet and percussion and includes a number of dramatic, almost theatrical elements. The contrast between these two works should make for an exciting and varied concert. Here are two sample recordings to whet your appetite: Durante, *Magnificat* https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YsadCm0bLsg&list=PLD-PIN2cqy5MnNttWHdn6tcG4HWyyJWxB Jenkins, *Te Deum* https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kexijL3yt7c We will also perform a couple of shorter works. Our first rehearsal will be Monday, August 24, at 7:00 p.m. at Wesley United Methodist Church in Bloomsburg. The concert will take place Sunday, November 15, at 3:00 p.m. I look forward to seeing you and beginning this new season together! If this will be your first performance with me, please come say hello so I can get to know you! Joe Stuligross, Artistic Director
